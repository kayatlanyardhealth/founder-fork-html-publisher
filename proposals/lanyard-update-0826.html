The call that got my attention in August came from EY-Parthenon's healthcare team. They advise a company doing about $150M a year selling into health systems and payers, and that company wants a credentialing partner. Three structures came up on the call, a referral partnership, an investment, or an eventual acquisition. I told them Lanyard is open to a partnership that could grow into something bigger and is not for sale today. They said two weeks for the client's answer. As of August 31 I had not heard it.

The rest of the month was diligence. Golden Angels in Milwaukee, about 70 angels writing $200K to $300K checks, put me through a full session on August 25. Bread and Butter, Helgen, SixThirty, and Mucker each took the pitch. SixThirty told me their LPs sit on the payer side, so that one is a weak fit and I appreciated the straight answer.

What I learned

I lost a customer this summer because I could not answer support questions fast enough while running everything alone. My first hire was a customer success specialist, not an engineer, and I will defend that order to anyone who asks. A practice that signed with you and cannot get a reply will leave before the software ever gets a chance to prove itself.. I already knew this from 15 years on the other side of the table and I still let it happen.

The raise

$250K of the $1M is committed on a SAFE, no lead yet. August put five funds and one angel group into the conversation, and Golden Angels is the furthest along. The note I heard on more than one call was to split recurring revenue from one-time enrollment revenue on the traction slide (fair, I had them blended). I am rebuilding that slide now.

Product

In August I built payer follow-up into the platform. Lanyard now keeps track of who to contact at each payer and follows up on open applications on a schedule, in the same format I used for 15 years, so nobody has to remember to chase a stalled one. A new Network view shows you, state by state, which payer networks a provider is already in. That view is the first piece of the provider data layer I want Lanyard to become (the enrollment work is how we earn the right to hold that data).

The rest of the month went to security and compliance, the groundwork a health system or payer will ask about before they connect to us. Stronger sign-in for every user, sensitive provider data locked down, the SOC 2 policies written, and safeguards so one practice cannot see another practice's information.

Team and Birmingham

The customer success specialist started in August. The credentialing operations role drew 80 applicants in its first two weeks. An executive assistant came on to run the shared client inbox and my calendar. On August 27 I sat down in person with Ambetter of Alabama's provider relations team, and they confirmed delegated credentialing is available to us on request.

Finishing gener8tor

The gener8tor Prosper Health Tech Accelerator wrapped with the showcase in Birmingham on September 1, and I pitched Lanyard on that stage to a room of investors, health system operators, and the people who have been around this company since the beginning. Twelve weeks of strategy sessions, investor swarms, mentor office hours, and pitch practice went into the version of the story I told that night, and the deck that came out of it is the one I am raising on now.

The program gave me three things I did not have in May: a direct line to the Alabama investor community, a set of operators who will pick up the phone when I have a question about a health system sale, and a much sharper answer to the question of how this scales. I owe Amanda, Isaiah, and the mentor bench a lot for that.

Where you can help. Introductions are the one thing I need this quarter, and I want to be specific about which ones.

Practices hiring clinicians this fall. Behavioral health groups, women's health, primary care, and physical therapy practices with 5 to 75 providers who are adding people between now and January. Every new hire has to be enrolled with payers before they can bill, and that is the moment a practice feels the problem. A group adding three therapists in October is a better intro for me than a hospital system.

Your portfolio companies. If you have a company in your portfolio that sells into practices or employs providers (a virtual care company, a staffing or locums platform, an EHR or billing tool, a behavioral health operator), I want to talk to whoever owns provider onboarding there. Two things can come out of it. Either they need enrollment done and become a customer, or they touch the same practices we do and become a referral partner.

Payer contacts. A provider relations or network management lead at a commercial plan or Medicaid managed care organization, in any state. I am building the payer side of Lanyard and I need more of these conversations, Blue Cross Blue Shield of Alabama first.

Health system operators who feel the delay. A medical staff office director or a VP of operations at a system where credentialing takes 60 days or more. I do not need the CIO. I need the person whose new physician sat unbilled for two months.

A blind intro is fine. Reply with a name and a line of context and I will take it from there, and I will tell you what happened.
